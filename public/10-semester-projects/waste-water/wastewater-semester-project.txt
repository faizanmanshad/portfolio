SEMESTER PROJECT – WASTEWATER / COMBINED SEWERAGE SYSTEM DESIGN USING EPA SWMM

As part of the Wastewater subject semester project, I worked on the hydraulic design and performance evaluation of a combined sewerage system for an approximately 102-hectare urban catchment near Markazi Jamia Masjid, Mall Road, Wah Cantt.

The project involved estimating wastewater flows, defining catchments and drainage parameters, developing the sewer network, and modelling the complete system in EPA SWMM. The design considered both sanitary wastewater and stormwater runoff so that the network could be assessed under normal as well as severe rainfall conditions.

A 20-year design population of approximately 3,835 persons was projected using geometric growth, and peak wastewater flow was estimated using Harmon’s peak factor. RCC sewer pipes were adopted with a Manning’s roughness coefficient of n = 0.013. The model used rainfall-runoff analysis, Dynamic Wave flow routing, Curve Number infiltration, junctions/manholes, conduits, subcatchments, and outfalls.

The system was evaluated under two rainfall scenarios:
• A typical rainfall event of about 30 mm/hr
• An extreme monsoon rainfall event of about 60 mm/hr

A major engineering challenge was the steep and undulating terrain of the catchment, where ground elevations varied from approximately 1590 ft to 1543 ft. This created difficulty in balancing two important hydraulic requirements: limiting excessive flow velocity in steep sections while maintaining sufficient self-cleansing velocity in flatter sections.

Initial simulations showed problems such as excessive velocities, conduit surcharging, and surface flooding at multiple locations. To improve the design, pipe slopes and diameters were adjusted, invert levels were revised, and drop manholes were introduced where large elevation differences occurred. The network was repeatedly analyzed and optimized in EPA SWMM until acceptable hydraulic performance was achieved.

The project included:
• Wastewater flow estimation and population projection
• Combined sewerage system layout and hydraulic design
• Subcatchment and stormwater runoff modelling
• EPA SWMM network modelling and simulation
• Pipe diameter, slope, invert level, and flow capacity checks
• Manhole and outfall configuration
• Velocity checks for self-cleansing and maximum allowable limits
• Surcharge and flooding assessment
• Dynamic Wave hydraulic routing
• Comparison of typical and extreme rainfall scenarios
• Iterative optimization of the sewer network
• Review of node depth, inflow, conduit flow, runoff, and outfall performance summaries

Following optimization, the final design achieved acceptable flow velocities within the intended operating range and eliminated the major surcharge and flooding problems identified in the initial simulations. The final system provided a hydraulically stable combined sewerage solution for the selected catchment.
